About

I came to security through the problems, not the theory.

Years of IT support in Uganda showed me that the technical fix was rarely the hard part — the hard part was the policy, the process, and the trust around it. That pulled me toward cybersecurity, and specifically toward governance, risk and compliance: the discipline of making systems trustworthy, not just functional.

Today I'm completing a master's in cybersecurity at Yeshiva University's Katz School while building at the intersection of security and software.

I don't just study frameworks — I build with them.

I designed an AI system that automates compliance documentation across SOC 2, ISO 27001 and NIST CSF, with a track for emerging AI-governance standards. I co-founded AXAM, an offline AI education platform that keeps student data on-device to meet FERPA requirements — it won Outstanding Impact in Science & Technology at Yeshiva's 2026 symposium. And I ship consumer software, like Bwi-Know, a live platform documenting African basketball statistics.

What connects all of it is a belief that good security is really about people: protecting them, earning their trust, and making the complex feel clear.

Flagship · Co-founded venture

AXAM — Offline AI Education Platform

An AI tutor that runs with no internet and no cloud — so student data never leaves the building.

An offline, AI-powered education platform for schools with limited connectivity, privacy constraints or focus challenges. It runs entirely on low-compute devices and never connects to the internet — acting as a Student Learning Companion (plain-language tutoring in algebra and English) and a Teacher's Toolbox (auto-generating lesson plans, handouts and differentiated materials that save teachers hours a week).

Role Co-Founder / Co-Entrepreneurial Lead and co-technical lead, on a three-person team of Katz School graduate students.

The hard part Because everything stays on-device and on the school's own network, AXAM is built to meet FERPA and U.S. Department of Education privacy requirements — nothing is sent to Google, OpenAI or any cloud. Customer discovery revealed that privacy, compliance and ease-of-use — not connectivity — were the real barriers schools faced. It's designed for settings where the internet is restricted for legal or safety reasons, including juvenile-justice and high-security environments.

Recognition Awarded Outstanding Impact in Science & Technology at the Katz School's 2026 Symposium on Science, Technology and Health. Developed through the NSF I-Corps program (Summer 2025).

Flagship · GRC tooling

Compliance Architect Gem

Turns weeks of compliance paperwork into days — and won't overstate its coverage.

An AI workspace I designed that automates compliance documentation across SOC 2, ISO 27001 and NIST CSF, generating it from a 76-control cross-walk I built by hand — with a dedicated AI-governance track for ISO 42001 and the NIST AI RMF.

Role Sole designer of the control mappings and framework logic.

The hard part The cross-walk itself. The same control often satisfies different framework requirements at different levels of specificity, so the real work was figuring out how one piece of evidence could legitimately cover multiple frameworks without overstating coverage — the distinction that makes compliance automation trustworthy rather than just fast. It's designed to compress the documentation timeline for organisations beginning their compliance journey.

Context Grew out of a cybersecurity internship writing SOC 2 / ISO 27001 policy; being extended as a graduate capstone.

Bwi-Know — Basketball Analytics Platform

Putting African basketball on a permanent, public record — because stats are documentation.

A live, deployed web platform documenting African league basketball, starting with Uganda: men's and women's standings, per-game leaders, player profiles, box scores, schedules, an auto-refreshing scoreboard and a news feed. Built as a one-stop archive and resource for coaches, players, writers, commentators and media — the raw material a sport needs to be understood and its story told.

Role Sole builder — data model through deployment.

The hard part Two things. First, domain mastery — I couldn't present statistics responsibly without understanding what they mean, so I studied the foundations of basketball analytics (including Dean Oliver's Basketball on Paper). Second, data access and granularity: in a country like Uganda, cumulative season totals are available but individual game-by-game detail is hard to reach — so a real part of the work is reconstructing and presenting that granularity, which is what makes stats useful for understanding a season rather than just summarising it.

Status Live. Game-by-game granularity and full live-score coverage are in progress.
